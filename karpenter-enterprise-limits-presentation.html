WHERE KARPENTER STOPS

Five walls that aren't configuration problems

01Nodes provisioned perfectly, utilization stays low
02Consolidation switched off for stateful workloads
03Spot adoption stalls after first interruptions

01
WALL 01 · OVER-REQUESTED PODS

Pods request far more than they use

Karpenter provisions based on what pods request, not what they actually use. No node autoscaler can fix padded requests — they just provision around the waste.

69%
of clusters over-provision CPU
79%
over-provision memory
8%
actual avg CPU utilization

Requests are set conservatively at manifest time. The cost of an OOM is visible; the cost of waste is not.

02
WALL 02 · CONSOLIDATION BREAKS STATEFUL

Consolidation gets switched off

Stateful and long-running workloads can't survive the disruption of standard eviction. Teams restrict or disable consolidation entirely to protect them — and efficiency stalls.

THE PROBLEM
Standard pod eviction terminates and restarts applications. For databases, streaming apps, and JVM workloads, this means downtime, data risk, and cold starts.
THE ANSWER
Container Live Migration moves running containers between nodes — preserving memory, process state, and TCP connections.
03
WALL 03 · SPOT ADOPTION STALLS

First interruption hits production,
Spot gets turned off

Karpenter treats all Spot pools equally and relies on AWS's standard 2-minute interruption warning. After the first few disruptions hit production, clusters quietly drift back to on-demand.

2 min
AWS interruption warning
30 min
Cast AI prediction window

Cast AI's ML model identifies at-risk nodes up to 30 minutes before interruptions — enabling graceful migration with minimal disruption.

04
WALL 04 · COST BLIND SPOTS

Nobody can say where the money goes

Costs are visible at the node level, but nobody can say which team or workload the money actually goes to. Karpenter has no awareness of Reserved Instances, Savings Plans, or negotiated discounts.

WHAT'S MISSING
• Cost attribution by namespace, workload, or team
• Reserved Instance and Savings Plan utilization tracking
• Real-time spend visibility across cluster fleets
• Showback and chargeback to engineering teams
05
WALL 05 · MULTI-CLOUD FRAGMENTATION

Different tooling, different logic, per cluster

Multi-cloud setups end up with Karpenter here, Cluster Autoscaler there. Different optimization logic per cluster means no unified visibility, no consistent policies, and no fleet-wide efficiency.

THE RESULT
Each cluster becomes its own optimization island. Engineering teams reinvent the same logic across providers. Savings achieved in one cluster don't transfer to others. The optimization burden scales linearly with cluster count.
